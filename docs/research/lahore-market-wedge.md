# Why would a Lahore restaurant leave foodpanda? The wedge, priced and named

Research ticket: [hedonarc/foodio#89](https://github.com/hedonarc/foodio/issues/89)

Foodio's strategic bets are (1) subscription pricing instead of per-order commission, (2) restaurant-owned delivery staff instead of a platform rider fleet, (3) short-video discovery. This doc checks each bet against primary sources: foodpanda's actual commission behavior, why local challengers (Cheetay, Airlift) died, the economics of restaurant-run delivery, and evidence that video drives orders in Pakistan.

## 1. foodpanda Pakistan's commission rates and restaurant disputes

foodpanda holds an estimated **99% share of Pakistan's food delivery market**, largely built on acquiring rival EatOye in 2015 ([Rest of World, 2022](https://restofworld.org/2022/foodpanda-stumbles-in-pakistan/)). That dominance is the backdrop for a documented, repeated pattern of commission disputes with restaurants:

- **September 2020 boycott.** The All Pakistan Restaurants Association (APRA) led a three-day boycott (Sept 15–17, 2020) after foodpanda tried to raise commission from **18% to 25–35%**. APRA argued this was "not viable" given restaurants' raw-material costs alone run ~50% of revenue. Over 20 eateries were cut off from the app for participating, per a list reviewed by _Dawn_ ([Dawn, 2020](https://www.dawn.com/news/1580703); [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40019329)). The boycott expanded beyond Karachi to the Lahore Restaurant Association, Lahore Restaurants Unity Association, and Islamabad Restaurant Association within a week ([Images/Dawn](https://images.dawn.com/news/1185808/karachi-restaurants-boycott-foodpanda-over-exploitative-practices-commission-hike)).
- **A second, distinct grievance in the same dispute**: foodpanda was pushing to eliminate restaurants' own delivery fleets and route all logistics through its own riders, and was asking for exclusivity clauses restaurants considered anti-competitive ([Dawn, 2020](https://www.dawn.com/news/1580703)). This is directly relevant to Foodio's bet #2 — restaurants were fighting _to keep_ their own delivery staff, not to get rid of them.
- **July 2021: Competition Commission of Pakistan (CCP) inquiry.** The CCP opened a formal antitrust inquiry into foodpanda following complaints of "abuse of dominant position," "exorbitant commissions," discriminatory pricing, and loyalty/exclusivity agreements ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2309344/ccp-launches-inquiry-against-foodpanda); [CCP press release](https://cc.gov.pk/home/viewpressreleases/7)).
- **2022 reporting confirms the commission stuck at the high end.** A foodpanda "home kitchen" operator told _Rest of World_ the platform takes a **35% cut** on his sales, even as foodpanda simultaneously cut rider pay and shrank delivery radii for lack of riders ([Rest of World, 2022](https://restofworld.org/2022/foodpanda-stumbles-in-pakistan/)).
- **Present-day (2025–2026) secondary sources** (restaurant-marketing/agency blogs, not primary) put the effective commission at **25–35%**, describing a base 25–30% plus a further ~5 points for better in-app visibility, plus 2–3% payment processing — i.e., a restaurant can lose roughly a third of order value ([Menuviel, 2025](https://blog.menuviel.com/foodpanda-fees-and-commissions-for-restaurants/); [OnlineOrder.pk](https://onlineorder.pk/foodpanda-commission-rate-pakistan/)). These are not foodpanda's own published rate cards — treat as directionally consistent with, but not confirmation of, the 2020–2022 figures.
- **Rider-side unrest reinforces the "platform squeezing everyone" narrative.** Riders in Lahore specifically protested outside the Lahore Press Club in July 2021 over pay cuts and working conditions ([Cutacut, 2021 — via search snippet, site unreachable directly]; corroborated by [Rest of World, 2022](https://restofworld.org/2022/foodpanda-stumbles-in-pakistan/), which reports thousands striking in March 2022 over repeated delivery-rate cuts). Long-time Pakistan CEO Nauman Sikandar Mirza left in February 2022 after seven years, coinciding with this unrest ([Rest of World, 2022](https://restofworld.org/2022/foodpanda-stumbles-in-pakistan/)).

**Evidence quality:** Strong. The commission-hike dispute and CCP inquiry are corroborated across _Dawn_, Business Recorder, Arab News, Express Tribune, and the CCP's own press release. The 2025/2026 "current rate" figures come only from restaurant-marketing blogs (Menuviel, OnlineOrder.pk) — directionally credible but not primary-sourced to foodpanda.

## 2. What happened to Cheetay and Airlift

- **Cheetay** (founded 2015 by brothers Ahmad Khan and Majid Khan) began as a food-delivery/errand platform and pivoted toward quick commerce. It moved toward shutdown starting late 2023 (mass layoffs) and was confirmed to be "mulling shutdown" by January 2024 ([Profit/Pakistan Today, Jan 2024](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2024/01/18/cheetay-mulling-shut-downg-dastgyr-and-others-look-towards-layoffs-as-startups-go-through-another-purge)). Reporting attributes the collapse to: a structurally high-burn quick-commerce model without matching demand in Pakistan, foodpanda's near-total dominance of the restaurant-aggregator segment it also competed in, and the broader 2023 global capital drought plus domestic inflation squeezing both costs and consumer demand ([Profit/Pakistan Today](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2024/01/18/cheetay-mulling-shut-downg-dastgyr-and-others-look-towards-layoffs-as-startups-go-through-another-purge); [Techlist](https://www.techlist.pk/tough-times-for-pakistani-startups-cheetay-faces-closure-dastgyr-and-others-consider-layoffs/)). Cheetay was _not_ primarily a foodpanda-style restaurant aggregator by the end — this is a caveat: it's adjacent evidence about the Pakistani delivery-startup funding environment, not a direct precedent for a subscription-priced, restaurant-fleet food app.
- **Airlift** (Pakistan's most-funded startup at the time, quick-commerce grocery) shut down July 12, 2022, citing the "global recession and recent downturn in capital markets" after a funding round it needed fell through — some investors reportedly told the company it would take over two months to wire committed money ([TechCrunch, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/07/12/airlift-shutdown/)). Contributing factors reported by _Rest of World_: Pakistan's food inflation (>17%) raised procurement costs while squeezing consumer spending, and Pakistan's low purchasing power meant Airlift couldn't charge delivery fees/margins comparable to Western peers ([Rest of World, 2022](https://restofworld.org/2022/inside-story-of-airlift-crash/); [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1699407)). Notably, Airlift itself said it was roughly 3 months from operating profitability when it died — the failure was a **financing-timing failure**, not proof the underlying unit economics were unworkable ([TechCrunch, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/07/12/airlift-shutdown/)).

**Evidence quality:** Strong for the proximate causes (funding-market timing for Airlift; burn/demand mismatch plus foodpanda dominance for Cheetay). Weaker as a direct analogy to Foodio, since neither company ran a subscription model or a restaurant-owned-fleet model — they are evidence about _capital discipline and market conditions_ in Pakistani delivery startups generally, not evidence against Foodio's specific bets.

## 3. Economics of restaurant-run delivery in Lahore

This is the weakest-evidenced of the four areas.

- **What's confirmed:** Before foodpanda pushed to centralize logistics, restaurant-owned delivery fleets were the norm and restaurants fought to keep them. A Kaybees director told _Dawn_: "When we started out with Foodpanda, it was simply them redirecting orders to us, which were then delivered by our fleet" — i.e., aggregator-as-order-router with restaurant-owned delivery is a proven, pre-existing operating model in the Pakistani market, not a novel one ([Dawn, 2020](https://www.dawn.com/news/1580703)). This directly supports Foodio's bet #2: restaurants already know how to run their own delivery, and many did until foodpanda took that function away from them.
- **What's partially confirmed:** foodpanda's own riders earn roughly **PKR 50,000/month on average** in Pakistan, ~76% above the reported national average wage ([Indeed.com aggregated salary data, cited via search](https://pk.indeed.com/cmp/Foodpanda/salaries/Delivery-Driver)). This is a useful benchmark for what a restaurant would need to pay to run competitive in-house riders, but Indeed's aggregated self-reported salary data is a weak primary source, and it doesn't include foodpanda's per-order/fuel-allowance structure or restaurant-side hidden costs (bike maintenance, insurance, turnover).
- **What is not found:** No restaurant-industry commentary, case study, or press source quantifying the total cost of a Lahore restaurant running 3-5 own riders versus paying foodpanda's ~30% commission on the same order volume. No source directly compares "commission saved" against "delivery payroll + vehicle costs" at the unit level.

**Verdict: largely unverified.** The _precedent_ (restaurants used to and can run their own delivery) is well sourced. The _economics_ (is it cheaper than a 30% commission once you carry payroll, bikes, fuel, insurance, and idle-rider time) is not demonstrated by any source found — this is the single biggest hole in the Foodio thesis as researched here, and should be treated as an assumption to validate with real restaurant partners, not a proven claim.

## 4. Evidence that short-video content drives orders in Pakistan

This is also weak. Search turned up Lahore-specific TikTok food-content activity (food bloggers, a themed "All YouTubers Restaurant" catering explicitly to influencer culture, discovery hashtags like "Bara Restaurant Lahore" and "Food Bloggers in Lahore") ([TikTok discover pages](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/food-bloggers-in-lahore?lang=en)), and general industry commentary that restaurants report a correlation between TikTok visibility and increased visits ([CloudKitchens blog](https://cloudkitchens.com/blog/tiktok-marketing-for-restaurants)). None of this is a controlled or quantified Pakistan-specific data point (no named restaurant reporting an order lift with a number attached, no survey of Lahore consumers, no platform-reported data).

**Verdict: unverified.** There is qualitative, anecdotal evidence that a TikTok/food-creator scene exists in Lahore and that restaurants participate in it, and generic (non-Pakistan) industry claims that video drives visits. There is no sourced, quantified evidence that short-video discovery converts to orders in the Pakistani market specifically.

## The three strongest sellable claims

1. **foodpanda has a track record of unilaterally raising commissions into the 25–35% range, provoking a multi-city restaurant boycott and a formal antitrust inquiry.** A subscription-priced alternative directly answers a grievance restaurants have already organized around. Source: [Dawn, Sept 2020](https://www.dawn.com/news/1580703); [CCP inquiry, July 2021](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2309344/ccp-launches-inquiry-against-foodpanda).

2. **Restaurant-owned delivery fleets are not a hypothetical for Foodio to prove out from scratch — they are the pre-foodpanda status quo that restaurants explicitly fought to preserve** when foodpanda tried to force all logistics through its own riders in 2020. Source: [Dawn, Sept 2020](https://www.dawn.com/news/1580703) (Kaybees director quote).

3. **foodpanda's own rider workforce is itself unstable** — Lahore riders protested pay cuts in July 2021, and a nationwide rider strike followed in March 2022, alongside CEO turnover — evidence the incumbent's delivery-fleet model carries real labor-relations and service-reliability risk that a restaurant-staff model may sidestep. Source: [Rest of World, 2022](https://restofworld.org/2022/foodpanda-stumbles-in-pakistan/).

## The claim the evidence does not support

**"Running your own delivery riders is cheaper for a Lahore restaurant than paying foodpanda's commission."** No source found quantifies this trade-off. We have a wage benchmark for foodpanda riders (~PKR 50,000/month, weakly sourced from aggregated Indeed data) and confirmation that restaurants used to run their own fleets, but nothing that nets out payroll, vehicle costs, insurance, and idle time against commission savings at realistic Lahore order volumes. This is the load-bearing economic assumption behind Foodio's bet #2, and it is currently unverified — it should be validated with real restaurant-partner numbers before being used as a pitch claim, not asserted as fact.
